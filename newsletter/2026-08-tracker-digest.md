# A committee voted for BPC-157. Almost nothing changed.

You're getting this because you asked to hear when the regulatory tracker moves.
This is the first one. It'll only ever arrive when something actually changes —
there's no schedule, because manufactured urgency is how the rest of this
industry talks to you.

One thing happened in July that's worth your attention, and it's being reported
badly almost everywhere.

---

## What happened

On **23–24 July 2026**, the FDA's Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee voted
to recommend six peptides for the 503A Bulks List — the set of substances
compounding pharmacies may legally use:

**BPC-157 · TB-500 · KPV · MOTS-c · Epitalon · Semax**

It declined to recommend emideltide (DSIP).

Three details matter more than the headline.

**The margins were narrow.** 8–6 with one abstention for BPC-157, TB-500 and
KPV. 7–5 for MOTS-c. These were not consensus votes.

**The committee overruled its own scientists.** FDA's career reviewers argued
against recommendation, saying they lacked the evidence to assess safety and
effectiveness.

**Their objection was about identity, not danger.** The reviewers' central point
was that there is no universally accepted chemical formula for these compounds.
Not "we think these are harmful" — *we cannot tell you what these are.*

---

## What it does not mean

A recommendation is not a rule change.

The committee does not bind the FDA. Nothing has been added to any list. Any
actual change requires formal rulemaking — a process that takes time and can
end differently than the vote suggests.

**Nothing about what you can lawfully buy changed on 24 July.** If you see a
seller citing this vote as validation — or worse, as approval — that is the
claim to distrust. It is the single most predictable piece of marketing to come
out of this meeting, and it was already circulating within days.

---

## Why the objection is the interesting part

The reason FDA's scientists balked is the same argument this site keeps making.

You cannot properly evaluate a substance whose identity is not pinned down. That
is true for a federal advisory committee reviewing a compound class, and it is
true for you holding a vial. A purity figure describes the sample that was
tested. It says nothing about whether the molecule is the one on the label, and
nothing about what else is in there.

The regulators hit the same wall you do. They just have better instruments.
